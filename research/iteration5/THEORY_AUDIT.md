# Stage 6.2 and Stage 10 (analytic part): theory audit

*2026-10-28. Everything here is either a standard result (labelled) or a short derivation given in full. Nothing here is claimed as new mathematics.*

## 1. Proposition 1 (paper, Section III)

**Statement.** For S = g(X) with g measurable: I(Y;A | S) = I(Y;A | X) + R(S), with R(S) = I(Y;X | S) − I(Y;X | S,A).

**Check.** Chain rule in two orders on I(Y; A, X | S):
I(Y;A | S) + I(Y;X | S,A) = I(Y;X | S) + I(Y;A | X,S).
Since S is a function of X, the σ-algebras generated by (X, S) and X coincide, so I(Y;A | X,S) = I(Y;A | X). Rearranging gives the identity. **Correct.**

**Conditions the paper should state.**
- All terms must be finite for the rearrangement. Y is binary, so each term is at most H(Y) ≤ log 2. This holds as written.
- The clause "if g is injective, R(S) = 0" needs X to be a measurable function of S. For an injective Borel map between standard Borel spaces the inverse on the image is Borel (Lusin–Souslin), so the clause holds for images and feature vectors as used here. One sentence suffices.
- The Bayes-risk equality E ℓ(Y, P(Y | S)) = H(Y | S) refers to the true conditional distribution, not a fitted model (§3).

## 2. What the identity does and does not establish

**Establishes:** an exact accounting. The metadata's value given a representation equals the value no image model can remove (I(Y;A | X)) plus a representation-dependent term R(S). R can take either sign.

**Does not establish:**
1. *Monotone attenuation.* For nested representations S1 = h(S2) (S1 coarser):
   Δ(S1) − Δ(S2) = R(S1) − R(S2),
   and this can take either sign:
   - Redundancy: X = A = Y (one fair bit). Then Δ(constant) = I(Y;A) = 1 bit and Δ(X) = 0. Refinement removes the value.
   - Synergy: X, A independent fair bits, Y = X ⊕ A. Then Δ(constant) = I(Y;A) = 0 and Δ(X) = H(Y | X) = 1 bit. Refinement creates the value.
   The paper's sentence "R(S) > 0 … added value then attenuates as S improves" describes the first case only and must be reworded.
2. *An ordering of the frozen ladder.* HSV, ResNet, ConvNeXt and ViT features are not functions of one another, so even data-processing inequalities do not order them; only image-only performance does, empirically.
3. *A mechanism.* A smaller Δ̂ at a stronger representation is compatible with R(S) shrinking because S captures image information that substitutes for A (redundancy). It is equally compatible with the score's loss shrinking for reasons unrelated to A (§4). The identity cannot tell these apart.

## 3. What Δ̂ estimates

For fitted second-stage models q0(y | s) and q1(y | s, a), on held-out data from the training distribution:

E[ℓ(Y, q0) − ℓ(Y, q1)] = I(Y;A | s) + KL(p(y | s) ‖ q0) − KL(p(y | s,a) ‖ q1),

with each KL averaged over the conditioning variables. Proof: E ℓ(Y, q) = H(Y | ·) + KL(p ‖ q) for log-loss; subtract.

So Δ̂ → I(Y;A | s) requires both models to be well specified and estimation error to vanish. Even then the target is I(Y;A | s) for the one-dimensional score s = h(S), not I(Y;A | S). Applying Proposition 1 with (S, s) in place of (X, S) gives
I(Y;A | s) = I(Y;A | S) + I(Y;S | s) − I(Y;S | s,A).
The two agree when s is sufficient for Y given S both with and without A. The paper states the misspecification caveat (limitation ii) but not the score-sufficiency one. The HAM10000 "all features + metadata" control bears on it for one dataset only.

## 4. Local form, a bound, and a decomposition (standard facts, used to frame Stages 3–6)

- **Local utility.** Δ(S) = E_S[ u(S) ] with u(s) = I(Y;A | S = s) = E_{A|S=s} KL(p(y | s,A) ‖ p(y | s)). For a calibrated fusion model, E[Δ_i | s_i, a_i] = KL(q1 ‖ q0) =: K_i. Gadgil, Covert and Lee (ICLR 2024, Lemma 1) give the same result for dynamic feature selection.
- **Bound.** u(s) ≤ H(Y | S = s), because conditional mutual information is at most conditional entropy. Hence Δ(S) ≤ H(Y | S). Metadata can help only where the image leaves the label uncertain, so an association between benefit and image-model entropy (registered H2) is expected and is a manipulation check, not a discovery.
- **Decomposition.** With η(S) = I(Y;A | S) / H(Y | S) ∈ [0, 1], the share of the remaining uncertainty that metadata resolve:
  Δ(S) = H(Y | S) · η(S),  log[Δ(S1)/Δ(S2)] = log[H(Y | S1)/H(Y | S2)] + log[η(S1)/η(S2)].
  Attenuation therefore splits into two parts. One is that the image resolves more cases (H falls); the other is that metadata resolve a smaller share of what remains (η falls), which is the signature of redundancy. Both factors are estimable: H(Y | S) by the image-only held-out log-loss and Δ by Δ̂, with the §3 caveats. The paper's ρ = Δ/I(Y;A) relates as ρ = η · H(Y | S) / I(Y;A).
  *Status:* the identity is trivial. Its use to apportion observed attenuation is an **exploratory** analysis, and it was proposed after HAM10000's aggregate E40 numbers had been seen.

## 5. The shift-null test (Stage 10, analytic part)

**What the reported p-value is exact for.** p = |{τ : M(τ) ≥ M(0)}| / |Ω| is the exact randomisation p-value under H0': *given the evidence map e, the mask's position is uniform over circular translations of the torus Ω.* Equivalently, the joint law of (e, m) is invariant to translating m. Under H0' the observed placement τ = 0 is exchangeable with every other τ, so p is exactly valid.

**Why that is not the hypothesis of interest.**
- Images are not tori. Circular shifts wrap the mask across the image border, producing placements no real image has.
- Lesions in dermoscopy are centred by acquisition. In HAM10000 masks the lesion covers 89% of the central block and 9% of the border (AUDIT.md §3). Any evidence map with a centre bias, for example from the network's receptive-field and zero-padding geometry, then has M(0) > M(τ) for most shifts, whether or not e depends on where the lesion is. H0' is false for reasons unrelated to the claim "evidence follows the lesion", so rejections are anti-conservative for that claim.
- Spatial autocorrelation is preserved by the shift, which is why the paper prefers it to pixel permutation. Preserving autocorrelation is necessary but not sufficient: the transformation group must also leave the joint law of (e, m) invariant under the null, and translations do not when both e and m are centred.
- Per-image p-values are summarised as "share of maps significant", a descriptive count with no multiplicity control. That is acceptable if stated as such.

**Justified alternative (to be run as E48).** Under the null H0'': *the evidence map does not depend on which lesion is in the image*, masks of other images are exchangeable with the image's own mask, given lesion area. Test statistic M_i(own mask) against M_i(mask of image j) for j drawn from images with similar lesion area (area deciles). This keeps both centre biases and the real mask geometry. Compare also with a fixed centred-Gaussian "evidence" map, which should be declared significant by the shift null but not by the swap null if the argument above is right. The relative statement in the paper (pixel permutation declares more maps significant than the shift null) remains true; the absolute shares of significant maps under the shift null should not be interpreted as evidence of lesion-specific attention until E48 is run.

## 6. Bearing of the interpretability analysis on the paper's question

The CAM analysis asks where the *image* model's melanoma evidence lies. It does not measure metadata utility. Its one link to the main question is indirect: missed melanomas are smaller lesions, and P2 (lesion diameter) loses most of its value to deep scores. Recommendation: move the CAM section to an appendix or a short "image-model behaviour" paragraph, and state that it does not bear on incremental metadata utility.
